# reout

### BTC-1 · live track record

BTC-1 is the first of the systematic trading programs developed and operated by reout. A program is
named by the asset it trades and a number: an improvement to it is a new version under the same name,
in the same record, and a different strategy is a new program with a record of its own. A program
that allocates among other managers' programs, rather than trading a market itself, is named MM,
for multi-manager, and a number; admitting and removing a manager are part of its record, a manager
it has removed stays in its history, and its record begins at its own inception. BTC-1
is a directional strategy on the BTCUSDT perpetual contract, long and short. Its signals come from a
self-supervised, memory-based model rather than from hand-written indicators; everything downstream, from execution to
this record, is automated. From record inception, every deployed version of the model is hashed and
anchored on Bitcoin before it trades. At record inception the program runs proprietary capital. Its record
begins at a date fixed in advance. This repository is the public record of the account that runs it. Its contents are produced
by a scheduled job that reads the exchange API with a read-only key, and each day's files are
hashed and anchored on the Bitcoin blockchain with OpenTimestamps.

## Contents of the record

The record consists of the program's daily returns and net asset value index, every external
cash flow and the combined cost of commissions and funding as shares of net asset value, the
monthly reports, and the program's position relative to the Risk Limits, which were anchored
before the record began. No currency amount is published.

## Production of the record

```
daily, after day end exchange API  (read-only key, GitHub Actions)
                        -> raw/      benchmark response, unmodified
                        -> data/     derived daily series
                        -> attest/   OpenTimestamps proof of the day's files

monthly, 3rd, 00:00 UTC  data/  -> reports/  monthly report (Markdown and PDF) and its proof
```

All figures are produced by scripts in this repository from the exchange responses received by
the workflow; none is entered by hand. Account responses, which would disclose the size of the account, positions or fills, are hashed
and anchored rather than published (Methodology, section 2). The daily workflow is `.github/workflows/pull.yml`, the monthly ones
`.github/workflows/report.yml` and `.github/workflows/transactions.yml`, and their execution logs
are public. The computation of each figure is specified in the Methodology. The only text
not generated by a script is the description of changes, incidents, review findings and
reconciliation differences, written at the time and carried into the monthly report alongside the
generated figures.

## Data files

The Methodology states what is published; this section says where. Returns and ratios are decimal
fractions, and the indices are 100 at record inception.

**`data/equity_daily.csv`.** One row per UTC day of the record. `date_utc` is the day the valuation
interval covers: a row dated D is struck at the mark that ends D, and its benchmark close is the
close of the daily candle for D. A missed valuation point has no row, and the next row's return
spans the gap. The first row is record inception: both indices are 100, and it has no `ret_twr`,
`ret_l1`, `flow_frac` or `ret_btc`.

| Column | Definition |
|---|---|
| `date_utc` | UTC day covered by the valuation interval |
| `mark_ts_utc` | Valuation point: the exchange's timestamp of its daily record |
| `flow_frac` | Net transfers of the interval as a fraction of net asset value at the previous valuation point |
| `ret_twr` | Daily time-weighted return |
| `nav_idx` | Index of `ret_twr` |
| `ret_l1` | Daily return divided by three, the maximum notional multiple (derived series) |
| `nav_l1_idx` | Index of `ret_l1` |
| `btc_close` | Benchmark daily close |
| `ret_btc` | Benchmark daily return |
| `version` | Program version in force at the valuation point |

**`data/transfers.csv`.** Every external cash flow: `ts_utc`; `amount_frac`, the flow as a fraction
of net asset value at the preceding valuation point, positive into the account; and `kind`, either
`transfer` or `credit` (a rebate or reward paid by the exchange, counted as a flow and not as a
return).

**`data/income_daily.csv`.** One row per UTC day: `realized_pnl_frac`, `costs_frac` (commissions and
funding combined) and `other_frac`, each as a fraction of net asset value at the previous valuation
point, with `other_types` naming the types grouped under `other_frac`.

**`data/monthly.csv`.** One row per calendar month: for the month and since record inception, the
time-weighted return, the risk statistics and the benchmark return; the money-weighted return since
record inception; and the position on each metric of the Risk Limits at the last valuation point
the row includes. The row of the month in progress is replaced each day until the month ends.

**`data/corrections.csv`.** Corrections to data values, append-only: date, file, column, published
value, corrected value and reason. A correction to a report is a separate file,
`reports/YYYY-MM.correction-N.md`.

**`raw/YYYY-MM-DD/btc.json`.** The benchmark response of the day, unmodified.

The endpoints and field names in use are those in `pull.py`, and each day's files are produced by
the version of that script in the same commit. The monthly report is generated by
`tools/monthly_report.py` from `data/` alone.

## Verification

```
ots verify attest/<date>-preregistration.md.ots       # rules anchored before the record began
sha256sum -c --ignore-missing attest/<date>.sha256       # published files match the day's manifest
ots verify attest/<date>.sha256.ots                   # the manifest is anchored on Bitcoin
```

Lines under `account/` and `archive/` are hashes of files the record does not carry, and
`--ignore-missing` passes over them: they are the exchange's responses, held back because they state
the account's size. A copy handed to a verifier under NDA is checked by hashing it and finding the
hash on the line of its day.

`ots verify` checks a proof against a local Bitcoin node. Without one, `ots --no-bitcoin verify`
names the block and the Merkle root the proof commits to, which can be compared with that block's
header from any source. A proof committed in the last few hours may still be pending.

The workflow file `.github/workflows/pull.yml` shows what was fetched and how it was transformed.

## Scope of disclosure

Currency amounts, including the size of the account, positions, fills, trade statistics,
parameters and the model's inputs are not published. The category of the strategy (systematic,
directional, single instrument, long and short; signals from a self-supervised, memory-based model) is stated. The investment thesis is available to verifiers under NDA. The
implementation is not published; an independent verifier under NDA may confirm the sealed
configuration against its hash. The Disclosure sets out what is published and what is not.
